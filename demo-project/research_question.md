# Research Question

_Demo walkthrough of the vibe-researching skill. This is a toy question with synthetic data, not a real Bao research project -- built to show the 7 steps running for real._

## Question

Does household income predict life expectancy in a population, and how large is the association?

## Claim

Higher income is associated with longer life expectancy. This is a comparative/correlational claim, not yet a causal one -- the model below will not by itself establish that income *causes* longer life, only that the two move together in this data.

## Model and why

Ordinary least squares regression of life expectancy on log-income. OLS because the outcome (age at death) is continuous and the question is about the average association, not a threshold or a classification. Log-income rather than raw income because income effects on health are widely reported as diminishing at higher incomes (an extra $10,000 matters more at low income than at high income) -- this is a modeling choice, not a neutral default, and is flagged as such in a decision card once the data is in hand and this can actually be checked.

## Assumptions

- Observations are independent of each other (no shared households or repeated individuals in the sample).
- The income-lifespan relationship is approximately linear once income is log-transformed (will be checked, not assumed).
- No major omitted confounder that drives both income and lifespan is being ignored well enough for a purely descriptive/associational claim (this would need much more to support a causal claim, which is why the claim above is stated as correlational).

## Falsification

The claim fails if the fitted slope on log-income is not statistically distinguishable from zero, or if it is negative, in this dataset.

## Reproducibility check

Needs: this repository's `data/raw/` file, Python with pandas + statsmodels, and this project's scripts run in numbered order. No external data source since this demo uses synthetic data generated by `01_generate_data.py` itself.
